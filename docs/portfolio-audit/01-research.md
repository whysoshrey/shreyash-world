## 1.2 Research findings

### What recruiters and hiring managers have said or measured
1. **Initial screens are very short; the reviewer looks at titles first.** In Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study, the initial résumé screen averaged 7.4 s, and recruiters scanned for job titles and subheadings. Cluttered layouts, multiple columns and long sentences performed badly. [R: https://csuglobal.edu/sites/default/files/blog-files/theladders-eyetracking-studyc2.pdf, https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/]
   *Caveat:* this measured résumés, not portfolio sites, and its methodology has been questioned [R: https://standout-cv.com/?p=10872]. Use it only as evidence that the *first scan* is fast and favours titles.
2. **Summary first, full case study second.** A design-recruitment writer who reviewed about 100 portfolios advises a summary at the top (problem, solution, outcome) for the recruiter, with the full study linked for the manager. In that piece's data, 54% of design managers spend 5–10 minutes on a portfolio, and 66% of recruiters source mainly through LinkedIn. [R: https://uxdesign.cc/ever-wonder-how-recruiters-look-at-your-design-portfolio-cc8dd1ecb698]
3. **State your role and contribution.** Indeed Design's recruiters ask for end-to-end examples with "your role" and "specific contributions" highlighted [R: https://medium.com/indeed-design/how-to-stand-out-in-a-ux-interview-495caf8ba482]. NN/g lists "clearly stating your role" and "skipping a content audit (broken links, missing content)" among its seven portfolio mistakes [R: https://www.nngroup.com/videos/7-portfolio-mistakes/].
4. **Brevity, and phones.** At an NH UXPA hiring panel, an agency hiring manager said he will not read a 10-page case study, and an in-house manager said he sometimes reviews portfolios on his phone [R: https://medium.com/theuxblog/what-do-recruiters-and-hiring-managers-look-for-in-a-ux-portfolio-2ed9e7c1c207].
5. **Red flags (survey, 100+ design leads, 2026, reported on LinkedIn):** very low visual quality (55%), hard to navigate or confusing structure (48%), obvious overuse of AI (43%), overly long or dense content (42%) [R: https://bg.linkedin.com/in/kalin-zdravkov, summarising UXfolio's State of UX Portfolios 2026; full report not inspected].
6. **Fashion-specific.** A Burberry hiring lead quoted by GlamObserver: a CV should work "as a business card", understandable at a glance [R: https://glamobserver.com/what-fashion-companies-expect-when-you-apply-and-during-a-job-interview/]. Fashion-business recruiters report the in-demand roles are merchandising, planning, marketing, digital commerce, retail operations and analytics, and that business candidates also benefit from a portfolio [R: https://glamobserver.com/?p=47124].
7. **Many reviewers only look *after* the CV, to confirm or reject.** In Reddit hiring threads (tech-leaning), hiring managers say they rarely open portfolio sites, or use them only to confirm a hunch or find a reason against [R: reddit snapshots, e.g. https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1t4gswl/snapshots/2026-05-05T19%3A04%3A38.345544Z]. **Implication [I]:** the site must survive a *verification* visit. Inconsistencies with the CV and unsupported claims are the main risks.
8. **Work authorization and location.** International CV guides advise stating eligibility or relocation clearly, because employers check it early [R: https://www.visualcv.com/blog/international-resume/, https://hays.nl/career-coach/cv-working-abroad]. University careers offices advise the opposite for students seeking sponsorship [R: https://drexel.edu/scdc/career-services/searching-for-job/international-job-seeking-tips]. **Evidence is mixed.** State base location, target regions and remote availability (all user-stated) and leave visa detail out.

## Phase 2 — Best-practice framework for this portfolio

Derived from the sources above plus standards (WCAG 2.2 AA; `prefers-reduced-motion`). Each principle is labelled by its basis.

1. **Role compression on the first screen** [R2, R5, R6]: name, the role(s) sought, one line on proof, and the three strongest numbers, visible without scrolling.
2. **Contact and CV always within one click** [R7, P1–P6]: email, CV (consistent with the site), LinkedIn, and location and availability.
3. **Summary → depth** [R2, R4]: every case study opens with a five-line summary (problem, my role, what I did, result, status) before the long sections.
4. **Explicit role and evidence status** [R3]: "my part" vs. the team's; measured vs. reported vs. modelled vs. concept.
5. **Curate by audience** [R5, creativebloq "drowning out your best work"]: lead with three to four flagship projects; offer paths by role (operations, merchandising and analytics, AI, brand).
6. **Consistency with the CV** [R7]: the dates, titles and figures on the site must match the CV it links.
7. **Scannable structure** [R1]: titles and dates first, short declarative lines, no dense paragraphs.
8. **Motion that serves wayfinding only** [WCAG 2.3.3; reduced motion]: no curtain or entry delay that holds content back; respect reduced motion.
9. **Accessible and fast** [WCAG 2.2 AA]: contrast, keyboard focus, alt text, no horizontal overflow, compressed media, nothing blocking the first paint.
10. **Content audit** [R3 NN/g]: no broken links, placeholders or dead `#` links.
